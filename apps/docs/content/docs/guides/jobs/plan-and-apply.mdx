Plan an environment, review the plan, and deploy that exact plan. Nothing is provisioned until you
deploy. To do the same from a terminal, see [Plan and apply](/reference/cli/plan-and-apply).

## Before you begin

- The project has a design on its **Architecture** canvas. See [Design a project](/guides/design-project).
- A cloud account and a Git provider are connected. See [Connectors](/guides/connectors).
- A runner is online for your organization. See [Runners](/guides/runners).
- Your role can plan and deploy the project.

## Steps

<Steps>

### Open the review
On the project's **Architecture** canvas, pick the environment, then open **Review and deploy** from
the changes bar at the foot of the canvas. The review lists your design changes. If it lists
**Issues to fix before planning**, fix them first.

### Run the plan
Select **Run plan**. Alethia saves the design and queues a `PLAN` job. The runner plans the
environment, runs the [verify gate](#verify-gate) on the plan, and prices it when it can (see
[Cost estimation](/concepts/cost-estimation)).

### Review the plan
When the plan completes, the review shows **What the plan would change**: each resource marked as a
create, change, replace or destroy, and the cost after this plan. The plan is **Current** while
the design and the environment have not changed since it ran. It is **Out of date** when the design
was saved after it, or another job ran on the environment.

### Deploy
Select **Deploy**. When the plan
destroys or replaces resources, you type the environment's name to confirm. Alethia queues a
`DEPLOY` job that applies the saved plan. The verify gate runs again before `tofu apply`.

</Steps>

A successful plan job also has a **Deploy** button on its page, which asks which runner to use. See
[Read a job](/guides/jobs/job-detail).

## Check that it worked

The `DEPLOY` job ends with `SUCCESS`. The runner installs ArgoCD (see
[GitOps with ArgoCD](/concepts/gitops-argocd)), and the console records the cluster, database and
cache endpoints on the environment and marks it active. The environment's cluster appears under
[Clusters](/guides/clusters).

## Verify gate

Between `tofu plan` and `tofu apply`, the runner runs [Elench](/concepts/elench) on the plan JSON.
Each control passes, fails, or is `not_evaluable` when the plan cannot prove it. A hard failure
stops the deploy before `tofu apply`, unless an [override](/guides/jobs/verify-override) waives
that control. Plan jobs carry the same report, in `verify_result` in the job's execution metadata.
The controls are listed in the [control catalog](/reference/control-catalog).

## How a deploy stays bound to its plan

- The `PLAN` job must have ended with `SUCCESS`, or the `DEPLOY` job fails.
- The runner downloads the plan's saved file and applies it. If it cannot fetch the file, it logs a
  warning and plans again from the deploy job's own snapshot.
- Jobs queued through the jobs API, which the CLI uses, carry a configuration hash. When both jobs
  have one and they differ, the deploy fails with `configuration changed since plan was generated`.
  Console jobs carry no hash, so the saved plan file is what binds them.

## Destroy an environment

A destroy has no plan step.

1. On the canvas, open the environment's settings and select **Destroy environment**.
2. Type the environment's name and confirm. If other environments are placed on its cluster, the
   dialog lists them in the order they are destroyed.

Alethia queues a `DESTROY` job, and the environment goes from destroying to destroyed.

<Callout type="warn">
A destroy removes every cloud resource of the environment and cannot be undone. The OpenTofu state
is kept, so you can deploy again, but data in databases is lost.
</Callout>

## Free plan limits

The free Hobby plan has two limits on jobs:

| Limit | Hobby | Pro | Enterprise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jobs running at once | 2 | 8 | No limit |
| Jobs you start in a rolling 24 hours | 25 | No limit | No limit |

A job over the concurrency limit waits in the queue. At the daily limit, the next plan, deploy,
destroy or scan is refused with an upgrade prompt, and the limit clears as older jobs leave the
window. A self-hosted console changes the daily number with `ALETHIA_FREE_DAILY_JOB_QUOTA`.

Jobs that Alethia runs for you, such as drift detection, cluster probes, reconciliation and
ephemeral-environment cleanup, never count and are never blocked. Kubeconfig requests do not count
toward the daily limit either. They have their own limit of 20 requests in 10 minutes for each
person in an organization, and their runner time counts toward your included runner minutes.

---
title: Bring your own Helm chart
description: Attach your own Helm chart from Git or an OCI registry, read its safety scan, and bind the workloads it deploys to your databases, caches, queues and secrets.
---

Deploy a Helm chart you already have. Alethia scans it, draws each workload it deploys, and lets you
bind those workloads to the infrastructure on your canvas. It never rewrites the chart: the chart
deploys unchanged, as one ArgoCD Application. What the chart may create, how it syncs and how to
remove it are in [Bring-your-own charts](/concepts/bring-your-own-charts).

## Before you begin

- Your operator turned on bring-your-own charts for your instance. The capability is opt-in, and is
  limited to charts you own on your own cluster.
- For a chart in Git, its [Git provider is connected](/guides/connectors/git-providers).
- For a chart in a private OCI registry, the registry is connected as a
  [chart repository](/guides/connectors/chart-repositories) on the environment. Alethia uses that
  credential to scan the chart and ArgoCD uses it to pull. A public chart needs nothing. A registry
  on an internal network also needs
  [`ALETHIA_HELM_REGISTRY_ALLOW_PRIVATE`](/guides/runners/self-operated#environment-variables) on
  the runner.
- Charts in Amazon ECR cannot be scanned yet. GitHub Container Registry, GitLab, Docker Hub,
  Scaleway and other OCI registries work.

## Attach the chart

1. On the canvas, press **⌘K** and choose **Bring your own Helm chart** in the **Sources** group.
2. On **Source**, choose **Git repository** or **OCI registry**.
3. Describe where the chart is:

   | Source | Steps |
   | --- | --- |
   | Git | **Repository**, then **Chart path** (such as `charts/payments`), then **Ref**: a branch, tag or commit |
   | OCI | **Registry**: the `oci://<host>/<namespace>/<chart>` reference, such as `oci://ghcr.io/acme/charts/payments`. Then **Ref**: the chart version, or `*` for the latest release. |

4. On **Review**, set the namespace and your values, as schema-checked fields or raw
   `values.yaml`. Leave the namespace empty to get `byo-<chart id>`, such as `byo-payments`.
5. Select **Attach chart**.

The namespace must be your own. The form refuses `default`, `kube-system` and any other `kube-`
name, `argocd`, and the namespaces of the platform's controllers and add-ons, such as
`cert-manager`, `external-secrets-operator`, `ingress-nginx` and `monitoring`.

## Read the safety scan

Attaching starts a scan. The runner fetches the chart, then renders it with `helm template` in a
sandbox with no network and no secrets, and the [Elench](/concepts/elench/architecture) engine
checks the manifests. It looks for privileged containers, host access, wildcard RBAC, missing
resource limits and mutable image tags.

1. Select the chart's card.
2. Read the verify report in the inspector.

The registry credential is used only to fetch, outside the sandbox, so the chart never sees it.

## Bind a workload to your infrastructure

From the same render, Alethia draws each workload the chart deploys as a dashed card under the
chart. Each card shows the workload's kind (Deployment, StatefulSet, DaemonSet, CronJob or Job),
image, ports, environment variable names, resources and replicas. Values are never captured, so a
secret in the chart's manifests is never read into Alethia. The cards refresh on every scan, and
never deploy anything themselves.

1. Select a workload card.
2. Connect it to a database, cache, queue or secret, the same way as a
   [service](/guides/design-project/services).
3. Check the **value-path** each binding writes to, such as `postgresql.auth.existingSecret`.
   Alethia suggests it from the chart's conventions. Correct it if your chart differs.

Endpoints and ports are written into the chart's values as plain values. Credentials are written as
a reference to a Kubernetes Secret that the cluster provisions, never as a value in your chart or in
Git. A chart with no way to accept an existing Secret is flagged rather than given a credential.

## Override replicas and environment

In the same inspector, you can set:

- **Replicas**, to override the rendered count.
- **Environment**, to add or override environment variables.

Like bindings, overrides are written into the chart's values at the inferred or corrected value-paths
(`replicaCount`, `image.tag`, `*.existingSecret`). Your corrections win over the inferred paths and
survive a new scan.

## Check that it worked

After the deploy, the chart's ArgoCD Application is **Synced** and **Healthy**, in the namespace you
chose:

```bash
kubectl get pods -n byo-payments
```

If the **Deploy** tab warns that the chart was skipped or moved, see
[Troubleshoot design and GitOps](/troubleshooting/design-project#a-chart-is-skipped-because-of-its-namespace).

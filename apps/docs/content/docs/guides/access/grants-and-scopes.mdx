---
title: How grants and scopes work
description: The four parts of a grant, how a grant flows down from an organization to its projects, and how an explicit deny makes an exception.
---

Decide what grant to add. A grant is the unit of access, and it has four parts:

| Part | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| **Who** | A member, or a team (everyone on it) |
| **What** | A role, or a single permission such as `project:deploy` |
| **Where** | The whole organization, or one resource: a project, a runner or a cloud identity |
| **Effect** | Allow (the default), or an explicit deny |

You add grants in [Grant access](/guides/access/access-portal).

## Before you begin

- The organization is on the Enterprise plan for grants and teams. On the free plan, you are the only
  owner and already hold every permission.

## Scope flows down

Projects sit under the organization. Runners, cloud identities and connectors sit directly under the
organization too.

- A grant on the organization applies to every resource in it.
- A grant on one project applies to that project only.

So "let Dana view this one project" is one grant on that project.

## A deny always wins

A deny makes an exception to broad access, such as "Dana can view every project except this one".

- A deny beats any allow, direct or inherited. The most specific grant does not win: any deny that
  covers the resource refuses access.
- A deny flows down like an allow. A deny on the organization excludes all its projects; a deny on one
  project excludes only that project.

The rule: access is allowed when an allow covers the resource, directly or through its organization,
and no deny covers it.

### Example: everything but one project

1. Allow the `viewer` role to Dana on the organization. Dana can view every project.
2. Deny the `project:view` permission to Dana on project `S`.
3. Dana sees every project except `S`.

## Role or single permission

- **A role** is a bundle of permissions. Use a built-in role, or a
  [custom role](/guides/access/custom-roles) for an exact set. Changing the role changes every grant
  that uses it.
- **A single permission** is one `resource:action`. Use it for a one-off, and for a precise deny, such
  as `project:view` on `S` and nothing more.

## Check that it worked

The grant appears in **Settings → Access** with its principal, effect, role or permission, and scope.
Its **Effective reach** shows what it covers.

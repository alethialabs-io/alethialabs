Every piece of runner work, such as a plan, a deploy, a destroy, a drift check or a scan, is a job in a
Postgres queue. Runners claim jobs atomically, run them and report the result, so the console and the
CLI never wait on the work itself.

## How it works

### Queuing

You plan, deploy or destroy in the console or the CLI, and Alethia adds a `QUEUED` job with the
environment's frozen [configuration snapshot](/concepts/provisioning-pipeline#2-snapshot). Connecting
a cloud account is not a job: the control plane does it. The job types are listed in
[Job types](/guides/jobs/job-types).

### Claiming

A runner learns that a job is claimable from its wake stream, or from a 30-second poll when the
stream is down, and asks for one. The database picks the next job with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`: a
row another runner has locked is skipped, not waited on, so two runners can never claim the same job.

The pick also respects:

- **Priority.** Higher priority first, then oldest first.
- **Cloud.** A runner gets only jobs for the clouds it serves, and only jobs its cloud identity allows.
- **Self-operated jobs.** A job marked for a self-operated runner, such as a Hetzner job whose token
  stays in your runner, is never given to a managed runner.
- **Fairness and limits.** On managed runners, organizations share capacity fairly, and each plan's
  limit on jobs running at once applies. Extra jobs wait; they are not refused.
- **One job per environment.** Jobs on one environment run one at a time.

### Running and reporting

The runner sets the job `PROCESSING`, mints a credential, runs it, streams logs, and reports `SUCCESS`
or `FAILED` with its results. For a deploy, the control plane then writes the cluster and database
endpoints onto the environment. See [Job execution](/concepts/runner/job-execution).

### Failure recovery

A runner refreshes its liveness about every 10 seconds; after 45 seconds of silence it is marked
offline. A background sweep then recovers its job when:

- it was claimed over 15 minutes ago and its runner has sent no heartbeat for 5 minutes, or
- its runner is alive but the job made no progress for 30 minutes.

A recovered job goes back to `QUEUED` for another runner, which reads the state and continues. Two
cases fail instead:

- **The apply had started.** A retry would run the apply a second time, so the job fails with a
  message saying cloud resources may have changed. Check the environment before you deploy again.
- **It reached 5 attempts.** A job whose runner died or stalled five times fails, to protect the queue.

## What it does not do

- It does not run one job on two runners.
- It does not retry an apply that may have started.
- It does not push job status to the browser. Status is polled; see
  [Real-time updates](/concepts/realtime#poll-everything-else).

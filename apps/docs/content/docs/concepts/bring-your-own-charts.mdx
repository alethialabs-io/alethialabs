# Bring Your Own Charts

Alethia's add-on marketplace installs charts from public Helm registries. **Bring-your-own charts**
let you install a Helm chart from **your own git repository** instead, alongside marketplace add-ons
in the same cluster.

Your chart is not Alethia's code, so it runs under a deliberately narrower contract than a
marketplace add-on. This page is that contract: what gets deployed, what your chart is allowed to
create, what Alethia will and will not do to it afterwards, and how to remove it.

<Callout type="info">
  **A BYO chart is a Helm chart in git.** It is a different thing from
  [Bring Your Own IaC](/docs/concepts/bring-your-own-iac), which points an environment at your own
  **OpenTofu root module** and provisions the infrastructure itself. They can be used together;
  neither requires the other.
</Callout>

## What happens when you enable one

Each deploy, Alethia:

1. creates the destination **namespace**, because your chart is not permitted to create one itself
   (see [What your chart may create](#what-your-chart-may-create));
2. registers a **repository credential** for your chart's repo, in a Secret scoped to that repo
   alone — no BYO chart can read another's token, or the apps repo's;
3. applies a hardened **ArgoCD AppProject** that confines the chart; and
4. applies the **Application**, which then syncs on its own.

Nothing about this is a one-time setup. The Application is re-applied on every deploy, so a change
to any of the above takes effect on your next deploy rather than needing the add-on removed and
re-added.

## A push to the tracked ref deploys

Your chart is attached at a git **ref**, and a ref is usually a branch — the default is `HEAD`.
ArgoCD watches that ref, so **a commit to it deploys, on its own, without an Alethia deploy and
without an approval step.** That is ordinary GitOps, and for most people it is the point: your
chart repo is the source of truth and the cluster follows it.

It is worth being deliberate about, though, because it means write access to that branch is
effectively deploy access to that namespace.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Attach a tag or a commit SHA if you want deploys to be explicit.** The `ref` accepts any git
  revision, so pinning it to `v1.4.2` or a full SHA means the cluster changes only when you change
  the ref — pushing to your branch then does nothing until you do. Use a branch when you want
  continuous delivery; use a tag or SHA when you want a release step.
</Callout>

What a push **cannot** do is escape the chart's confinement — every sync is validated against the
same hardened project, so a new commit is held to exactly the rules in
[What your chart may create](#what-your-chart-may-create).

## Sync behaviour

Your chart is deployed automatically. Two things Alethia deliberately does **not** do:

| | Marketplace add-on | Your chart |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Deployed automatically | yes | **yes** |
| Prune — delete resources no longer declared | yes | **no** |
| Self-heal — revert changes made in the cluster | yes | **no** |

**Prune is off, so a resource you delete from your chart keeps running.** If you remove a Deployment
from the chart and deploy, the old Deployment is still there. This is deliberate: Alethia will not
delete a workload of yours because your chart stopped mentioning it. To remove it, either delete it
yourself with `kubectl`, or [disable the add-on](#removing-a-chart), which removes everything.

**Self-heal is off, so changes you make in the cluster stick.** If you `kubectl edit` a resource
your chart owns, ArgoCD reports the Application `OutOfSync` and leaves your change alone. That is
what you want while debugging; it also means an `OutOfSync` BYO chart is informational, not an
error.

To put it back, you have to give ArgoCD something to do — **a deploy on its own will not revert it**,
because the chart is already at the revision it is meant to be at and there is nothing to sync. Any
of these works:

- push a commit to the tracked ref, which produces a new revision to sync;
- delete the edited resource, and the next sync recreates it from the chart;
- sync the Application from ArgoCD directly, which reapplies the current revision.

## What your chart may create

The AppProject Alethia applies is default-deny. Your chart may create **namespaced** resources in
its **own destination namespace** and nothing else:

- **No cluster-scoped resources.** No `Namespace`, `ClusterRole`, `ClusterRoleBinding`,
  `CustomResourceDefinition`, `StorageClass`, or any other cluster-scoped kind. A chart that
  declares one fails its sync with a message naming the resource.
- **No `Role`, `RoleBinding` or `ServiceAccount`,** even namespaced ones — a chart that could mint
  its own identity could grant itself more than this project allows.
- **Only the repositories you declared.** The project is locked to your chart's repo, so an
  Application cannot be re-pointed at a source you did not configure.
- **Only its own namespace.** The project's destinations list that namespace and no other.

<Callout type="warn">
  **If your chart needs a cluster-scoped resource, it cannot be a BYO chart as-is.** The usual
  answers are to split the cluster-scoped part out and have an operator apply it once, or to request
  the capability as a marketplace add-on. Relaxing the project for one chart would relax it for
  every chart in the environment.
</Callout>

## Removing a chart

Disabling the add-on deletes its Application, and the Application carries ArgoCD's
`resources-finalizer.argocd.argoproj.io` finalizer — so deletion **cascades to everything the chart
created**, including resources that prune would have left behind. The namespace Alethia created for
it remains.

So the two removal paths differ, and the difference is worth remembering:

- **Remove a resource from the chart** → it keeps running (prune is off).
- **Disable the add-on** → everything the chart created is deleted.

## Troubleshooting

**The Application is `OutOfSync` and I did not change anything.** Some charts render a value that
differs on every render — a generated password, a timestamp, a self-signed certificate. ArgoCD
compares each render against the cluster, so such a chart is permanently `OutOfSync`. Pin the value
in your chart, usually through an `existingSecret`-style option, and it settles.

**The Application is `Missing`.** Its resources do not exist. Check the sync result in ArgoCD: the
most common causes are a resource the project forbids (see
[What your chart may create](#what-your-chart-may-create)) and a chart path that does not exist at
the configured revision.

**The Application is `Progressing` and stays there.** ArgoCD is waiting for a workload to become
ready. Check the pods: a pod stuck `Pending` with `Insufficient memory` or `Insufficient cpu` is
asking for more than any node in your cluster can give it, which no amount of waiting fixes — lower
the request or provision a larger node type.

# Bring-your-own charts

Alethia's add-on marketplace installs charts from public Helm registries. **Bring-your-own charts**
let you install a Helm chart from **your own git repository** instead, alongside marketplace add-ons
in the same cluster.

Your chart is not Alethia's code, so it runs under a deliberately narrower contract than a
marketplace add-on. This page is that contract: what gets deployed, what your chart is allowed to
create, what Alethia will and will not do to it afterwards, and how to remove it. To attach a chart,
scan it, and bind its workloads, follow [Bring your own Helm charts](/guides/design-project/bring-your-own-charts).

<Callout type="info">
  **A BYO chart is a Helm chart in git.** It is a different thing from
  [bring-your-own IaC](/concepts/bring-your-own-iac), which points an environment at your own
  **OpenTofu root module** and provisions the infrastructure itself. They can be used together;
  neither requires the other.
</Callout>

## What happens when you enable one

Each deploy, Alethia:

1. creates the destination **namespace**, because your chart is not permitted to create one itself
   (see [What your chart may create](#what-your-chart-may-create)), and sets its admission rules
   (see [What the namespace refuses](#what-the-namespace-refuses));
2. registers a **repository credential** for your chart's repo, in a Secret scoped to that repo
   alone — no BYO chart can read another's token, or the apps repo's;
3. applies a hardened **ArgoCD AppProject** that confines the chart; and
4. applies the **Application**, which then syncs on its own.

A chart from an **OCI registry** follows the same contract. The one difference is step 2: its
credential is the one from your registry connection, not a git token. An OCI chart deployed before it
followed this contract moves to the hardened project on its next deploy, and the **Deploy** tab says
so: a resource the project does not permit then fails the chart's sync, and what the chart created
earlier outside the project is left in place.

Nothing about this is a one-time setup. The Application is re-applied on every deploy, so a change
to any of the above takes effect on your next deploy rather than needing the add-on removed and
re-added.

## Where your chart runs

Each chart runs in a **namespace of its own**. If you do not choose one when you attach the chart,
the namespace is `byo-` followed by the chart's id: a chart named `payments` runs in `byo-payments`.

Some namespaces are reserved for the platform, and a chart that targets one is refused:

- the Kubernetes system namespaces, such as `kube-system`;
- the namespaces where the platform's own controllers and the marketplace add-ons run;
- `default`, where the platform runs your environment's own services, with their Secrets and
  service accounts.

When you attach a chart, the namespace you choose cannot start with `byo-` either, unless it is the
chart's own: that prefix is kept for the namespace each chart gets by default. A chart you attached
before this rule keeps its namespace, also when you attach it again without changing it. If that
namespace is the default namespace of another chart in the same environment, that other chart keeps
it: your chart is not deployed, and the **Deploy** tab names both and asks you to attach yours again
with another namespace.

### A chart that was attached in `default`

Before this rule, a chart attached with no namespace ran in `default`. Such a chart, and a chart
attached with `default` itself, moves to its own namespace on the environment's next deploy:

1. The deploy shows a warning that names the chart and its new namespace.
2. ArgoCD deploys the chart into the new namespace, and the chart's project now permits only that
   namespace.
3. The chart's binding secrets are removed from `default` and created again in the new namespace.
4. **What the chart created in `default` stays there.** Its project no longer permits `default`, so
   ArgoCD no longer updates or deletes those resources, also when you remove the chart, and they keep
   running in the platform's namespace until you delete them. Delete them yourself when the chart runs
   in its new namespace. ArgoCD marked each one with an
   `argocd.argoproj.io/tracking-id` annotation that starts with `addon-<chart id>:`.

Nothing else in `default` is changed. To keep the new namespace in the chart's settings, attach the
chart again with that namespace or one of your own.

## A push to the tracked ref deploys

Your chart is attached at a git **ref**, and a ref is usually a branch — the default is `HEAD`.
ArgoCD watches that ref, so **a commit to it deploys, on its own, without an Alethia deploy and
without an approval step.** That is ordinary GitOps, and for most people it is the point: your
chart repo is the source of truth and the cluster follows it.

It is worth being deliberate about, though, because it means write access to that branch is
effectively deploy access to that namespace.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Attach a tag or a commit SHA if you want deploys to be explicit.** The `ref` accepts any git
  revision, so pinning it to `v1.4.2` or a full SHA means the cluster changes only when you change
  the ref — pushing to your branch then does nothing until you do. Use a branch when you want
  continuous delivery; use a tag or SHA when you want a release step.
</Callout>

What a push **cannot** do is escape the chart's confinement — every sync is validated against the
same hardened project, so a new commit is held to exactly the rules in
[What your chart may create](#what-your-chart-may-create).

## Sync behaviour

Your chart is deployed automatically. Two things Alethia deliberately does **not** do:

| | Marketplace add-on | Your chart |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Deployed automatically | yes | **yes** |
| Prune — delete resources no longer declared | yes | **no** |
| Self-heal — revert changes made in the cluster | yes | **no** |

**Prune is off, so a resource you delete from your chart keeps running.** If you remove a Deployment
from the chart and deploy, the old Deployment is still there. This is deliberate: Alethia will not
delete a workload of yours because your chart stopped mentioning it. To remove it, either delete it
yourself with `kubectl`, or [disable the add-on](#removing-a-chart), which removes everything.

**Self-heal is off, so changes you make in the cluster stick.** If you `kubectl edit` a resource
your chart owns, ArgoCD reports the Application `OutOfSync` and leaves your change alone. That is
what you want while debugging; it also means an `OutOfSync` BYO chart is informational, not an
error.

To put it back, you have to give ArgoCD something to do — **a deploy on its own will not revert it**,
because the chart is already at the revision it is meant to be at and there is nothing to sync. Any
of these works:

- push a commit to the tracked ref, which produces a new revision to sync;
- delete the edited resource, and the next sync recreates it from the chart;
- sync the Application from ArgoCD directly, which reapplies the current revision.

## What your chart may create

The AppProject Alethia applies is default-deny. Your chart may create the **namespaced** kinds in
the table below, in its **own destination namespace**, and nothing else. A chart that declares any
other kind fails its sync, and the sync result names the resource.

| Kind | API group | Why a chart may create it |
|---|---|---|
| `Deployment` | `apps` | The usual stateless workload. |
| `StatefulSet` | `apps` | A workload with a stable identity and its own volumes. |
| `DaemonSet` | `apps` | One pod on each node. Pod Security refuses its pods access to the node (see [Pod Security](#pod-security)). |
| `ReplicaSet` | `apps` | What a `Deployment` manages. A chart can also declare one directly. |
| `Job` | `batch` | Work that runs to completion, Helm hook jobs included. |
| `CronJob` | `batch` | A `Job` on a schedule. |
| `Pod` | core | A single pod, as a Helm test hook declares. |
| `ConfigMap` | core | Configuration that is not secret. |
| `Secret` | core | Your chart's own secrets. The secrets a binding gives your chart are created by the runner. |
| `PersistentVolumeClaim` | core | A claim on storage from the cluster's storage classes. |
| `Service` | core | A stable address for your chart's pods. |
| `Ingress` | `networking.k8s.io` | HTTP routing through the cluster's ingress controller. |
| `HorizontalPodAutoscaler` | `autoscaling` | Scales a workload in the namespace as its load changes. |
| `PodDisruptionBudget` | `policy` | Limits how many pods in the namespace a node drain can evict at once. |

The API group is part of the match. An `Ingress` from the old `extensions` group, for example, is
not on the list.

Some kinds are left out on purpose:

- **No cluster-scoped resources.** No `Namespace`, `ClusterRole`, `ClusterRoleBinding`,
  `CustomResourceDefinition`, `StorageClass`, or any other cluster-scoped kind.
- **No `Role`, `RoleBinding` or `ServiceAccount`,** even namespaced ones. A chart that could mint
  its own identity could grant itself more than this project allows.
- **No secret-sync resources.** No `ExternalSecret`, `SecretStore`, `PushSecret`, or any other kind
  from the `external-secrets.io` API groups, generators included. A chart reads only the secrets
  its [bindings](/guides/design-project/bring-your-own-charts#binding-a-chart-to-your-infrastructure)
  give it, and the runner creates those secrets for it. Each deploy also deletes any such object
  in the chart's namespace that no binding accounts for, and the Deploy tab names every object it
  deletes.
- **No kinds that an operator serves,** such as a cert-manager `Certificate`, a Prometheus
  `ServiceMonitor` or an ArgoCD `Application`. What such an object can make its operator do depends
  on that operator, not on this project.
- **No `Endpoints`, `EndpointSlice`, `ResourceQuota` or `LimitRange`.** A `Service` selects its own
  backends, and the namespace's limits are the platform's to set.
- **No `NetworkPolicy`.** A policy applies to every pod it selects in the namespace, and nothing
  guarantees that the namespace holds only your chart.

Two more rules apply to every sync:

- **Only the repositories you declared.** The project is locked to your chart's repo, so an
  Application cannot be re-pointed at a source you did not configure.
- **Only its own namespace.** The project's destinations list that namespace and no other.

### Asking for another kind

If your chart needs a namespaced kind that is not in the table, open a support case from
**Support** in the console. Name the kind and its API group, the chart that uses it, and what the
chart uses it for. A kind added to the list applies to every bring-your-own chart, so each
addition is reviewed on its own. Until then, turn that part of the chart off, usually
through a value, or apply it yourself outside the chart.

<Callout type="warn">
  **If your chart needs a cluster-scoped resource, it cannot be a BYO chart as-is.** The usual
  answers are to split the cluster-scoped part out and have an operator apply it once, or to request
  the capability as a marketplace add-on. Relaxing the project for one chart would relax it for
  every chart in the environment.
</Callout>

## What the namespace refuses

The project above decides which **kinds** your chart may create. It cannot see the fields inside
them. The namespace's admission rules do that, at two levels.

### Pod Security

Each namespace carries Kubernetes [Pod Security Admission](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/security/pod-security-admission/)
labels, pinned to the Kubernetes 1.33 version of the standard:

| Mode | Level | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `enforce` | `baseline` | A pod that breaks the level is refused. |
| `warn` | `restricted` | A pod that breaks the level is created, with a warning. |
| `audit` | `restricted` | A pod that breaks the level is created, and the cluster's audit log records it. |

`baseline` refuses a pod that asks for access to its node: a privileged container, the node's
network, process or IPC namespace, a `hostPath` volume, a host port, or added Linux capabilities.
A pod that does not set any of these passes, also when it sets no security context at all. That
is why the namespace enforces `baseline` and not `restricted`: `restricted` also requires settings
such as `runAsNonRoot` and a seccomp profile, which many public charts leave to you.

The warnings are for the `restricted` level, so your chart sees what it would have to change to
pass it.

### Admission policy

A ValidatingAdmissionPolicy refuses four kinds of object in the namespace:

| Refused | Why |
|---|---|
| A `Service` of type `LoadBalancer` or `NodePort` | It answers outside the namespace and outside the cluster, and a load balancer is billed to your cloud account. Use `ClusterIP` and an `Ingress`. |
| A `Service` with `spec.externalIPs` | It captures traffic that the cluster's nodes receive for those addresses. |
| A `Secret` of type `kubernetes.io/service-account-token` | It stores a service account token that never expires. Your pods already receive short-lived tokens without it. |
| An `Ingress` annotation with `snippet` in its name | A snippet configures the ingress controller itself, which serves every namespace. |

The policy needs Kubernetes 1.30 or later. Every Kubernetes version Alethia provisions supports it.
On a cluster that does not, the deploy skips the policy and says so in its log, and Pod Security
still applies.

### Charts that were attached before these rules

Refusing in a namespace where a chart already runs would refuse that chart's next pod, for example
after a restart or a node drain. So the namespace's `alethia.io/byo-admission` label decides
whether the rules refuse or only report:

- **`enforce`**: Pod Security enforces `baseline`, and the policy refuses. A namespace that Alethia
  creates for a new chart starts here.
- **`warn`**: Pod Security does not enforce beyond the cluster's own default, and the policy only
  warns and records. A namespace that already existed starts here, and so does the new namespace
  of a chart that moved out of `default`.

<Callout type="warn">
  **On most clouds, a `warn` namespace has no pod-level enforcement.** "The cluster's own default"
  is `privileged` on Amazon EKS, Google GKE Standard, Azure AKS and Alibaba ACK, and that level
  refuses nothing. Only Talos clusters, which Alethia runs on Hetzner, enforce `baseline` in every
  namespace by default. So a chart in a `warn` namespace on those four clouds runs with no Pod
  Security refusal until its namespace is in `enforce`.
</Callout>

A deploy never changes a namespace's label once it is set. While a namespace is in `warn`, each
deploy checks what runs there against both levels, and the **Deploy** tab names the chart, the
object and the field that `enforce` would refuse. Pod Security can only check the pods that run at
that moment, so a pod of a `CronJob` between runs is not checked. Change the chart until the
warning goes away. When you set the label to `enforce`, the policy refuses from then on and Pod
Security enforces from the next deploy. Setting the label needs `kubectl` access to the cluster:
the console has no control for it yet.

## Removing a chart

Disabling the add-on deletes its Application, and the Application carries ArgoCD's
`resources-finalizer.argocd.argoproj.io` finalizer — so deletion **cascades to everything the chart
created in its namespace**, including resources that prune would have left behind. The namespace
Alethia created for it remains. ArgoCD deletes only what the chart's project permits, so resources a
moved chart left in `default` are not deleted (see
[A chart that was attached in `default`](#a-chart-that-was-attached-in-default)).

So the two removal paths differ, and the difference is worth remembering:

- **Remove a resource from the chart** → it keeps running (prune is off).
- **Disable the add-on** → everything the chart created is deleted.

## Troubleshooting

**The Application is `OutOfSync` and I did not change anything.** Some charts render a value that
differs on every render — a generated password, a timestamp, a self-signed certificate. ArgoCD
compares each render against the cluster, so such a chart is permanently `OutOfSync`. Pin the value
in your chart, usually through an `existingSecret`-style option, and it settles.

**The Application is `Missing`.** Its resources do not exist. Check the sync result in ArgoCD: the
most common causes are a resource the project forbids (see
[What your chart may create](#what-your-chart-may-create)) and a chart path that does not exist at
the configured revision.

**The sync fails and names a kind that is not in the table.** Your chart declares a kind that
bring-your-own charts can't create. Turn that part of the chart off, usually through a value, or
[ask for the kind](#asking-for-another-kind). Charts often render a `ServiceAccount` by default;
set the chart's `serviceAccount.create` value, or its equivalent, to `false`. If the chart was
attached before its kind was refused, the objects of that kind it created earlier stay in place, and
ArgoCD no longer manages them. Delete them yourself.

**The sync fails and names an `ExternalSecret` or `SecretStore`.** Your chart ships its own
secret-sync resource, which bring-your-own charts can't create. Turn that part of the chart off,
usually through a value such as `externalSecret.enabled: false`. Then declare a binding on the
chart's workload and point the chart at the secret the binding creates. Alethia reports such a
chart as not synced but does not yet say why: the runner reads each Application's health and sync
status, not the sync result that names the resource. Check that result in ArgoCD.

**The sync fails with `ValidatingAdmissionPolicy 'alethia-byo-admission-enforce' … denied request`.**
Your chart declares an object that the namespace refuses (see
[Admission policy](#admission-policy)). The message names the rule. Change the value that renders
the object, for example `service.type: ClusterIP`.

**A pod is refused with `violates PodSecurity "baseline:v1.33"`.** The pod asks for access to its
node (see [Pod Security](#pod-security)). The message names the field. Turn that part of the chart
off, usually through a value.

**The Application is `Progressing` and stays there.** ArgoCD is waiting for a workload to become
ready. Check the pods: a pod stuck `Pending` with `Insufficient memory` or `Insufficient cpu` is
asking for more than any node in your cluster can give it, which no amount of waiting fixes — lower
the request or provision a larger node type.

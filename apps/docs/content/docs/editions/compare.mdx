---
title: How Alethia compares
description: The plain differences between Alethia and Porter, Qovery and Spacelift, including where each of them is the better choice.
---

Alethia is a control plane you can host yourself. It designs Kubernetes clusters and the services
around them, provisions them with OpenTofu into your cloud account, and delivers applications with
ArgoCD. This page states how that differs from three tools people compare it with, and where each
of them is the better choice. Product details of other vendors change: check their own
documentation before you decide.

## At a glance

| | Alethia | Porter | Qovery | Spacelift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Control plane that provisions clusters and services and delivers apps with GitOps | Platform that builds and deploys apps onto a cluster in your cloud | Platform that provisions Kubernetes in your cloud and deploys apps onto it | Orchestrator that runs the infrastructure code you write |
| Who runs the control plane | You (open source), or Alethia Labs | Porter | Qovery | Spacelift, or you on its self-hosted offering |
| Who writes the infrastructure code | Alethia's templates, or your own (bring-your-own IaC) | The platform | The platform | You |
| App delivery | ArgoCD, from your Git repository | The platform's own pipeline | The platform's own pipeline | Not an app platform |
| Source code | AGPL-3.0, except `ee/` | Proprietary | Proprietary | Proprietary |

## How Alethia signs in to your cloud

On AWS, GCP and Azure, Alethia stores no cloud key: your account trusts the instance's OIDC issuer,
and each job gets a short-lived credential. On Hetzner, DigitalOcean and Civo, which have no such
federation, Alethia stores your API token encrypted. See [Security](/concepts/security).

## Porter

Choose Porter when you want to push a repository and get a running app with logs, metrics,
autoscaling and preview environments, and a hosted control plane suits you. Its app experience and
day-2 operations are more mature than Alethia's.

Choose Alethia when the control plane must run on your own infrastructure, when you want standard
ArgoCD rather than a platform pipeline, or when you need databases, caches, queues and DNS designed
with the cluster. If you stop using Alethia, the cluster, the OpenTofu code and the GitOps setup keep
working.

## Qovery

Qovery is the closest in shape: it also provisions Kubernetes in your account and deploys onto it.
Choose Qovery for its mature app platform and its range of clouds and bring-your-own clusters.

Choose Alethia when you must host the control plane yourself under an open-source licence, when you
want ArgoCD as the delivery layer, or when you want the managed data services in the same design as
the cluster.

## Spacelift

Spacelift runs plan and apply for infrastructure code you already have, across many tools
(Terraform, OpenTofu, Pulumi, CloudFormation, Ansible and more), with policy as code and drift
detection. Choose it for a large, existing infrastructure-as-code estate.

Choose Alethia when you want a working cluster and its services without writing the code first, and
GitOps delivery on top. Alethia also runs your own OpenTofu modules (see
[bring-your-own IaC](/concepts/bring-your-own-iac)) and detects drift (see [Drift](/concepts/drift)),
but it is not a general orchestrator for every infrastructure tool.

## Where Alethia is behind

- It is a young product, with a smaller community and fewer integrations than all three.
- Day-2 application operations (logs, metrics, rollbacks) are thinner than Porter's and Qovery's.
- Its own templates cover AWS, GCP, Azure and Hetzner.
